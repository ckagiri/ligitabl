Frequently Asked

Everything about swaps, entering the competition, and how the season works.

Getting started

Everyone who enters the competition builds a Premier League table prediction — the order they think the 20 teams will appear after the upcoming gameweek has been played. They do this by tapping two teams to swap them, and submitting their prediction.

The real league table is then used to assign points to every player's prediction for that gameweek. A gameweek is a round of fixtures — the same idea as "Gameweek" in Fantasy Premier League.

Swaps during the season

One swap every 24 hours. The clock is rolling, not fixed to a time of day — it starts counting down the moment you use a swap, and resets once that 24 hours is up.

Each time a new gameweek opens, you get an additional swap on top of your regular allowance — useful if you need to reposition more of your table than a single swap allows.

Scoring & grades

You start each gameweek with 200 points. For every team, you lose 1 point for each position your prediction was off by:

Prediction Actual Points Hit
Team A in 1st Team A in 3rd -2
Team B in 20th Team B in 15th -5
Team C in 10th Team C in 10th -0

Your score is 200 minus your total position errors across all 20 teams. Once a new gameweek starts, the previous gameweek's score is frozen and the next one begins fresh at 200 — your cumulative score across all gameweeks is what the leaderboard ranks.

Every gameweek score earns a grade — S Pinnacle at a perfect 200, then A+ Legend (195–199), A Elite (190–194), A- Expert (185–189), B+ Proficient (180–184), B Advanced (170–179), C+ Competent (160–169), C Novice (145–159), and D Aspirant below 145. Even an off week still earns a grade to chase.

A zero is a perfect prediction — a team you placed in its exact final position, 0 positions off. Zeroes matter beyond the points they save you: they're also the first tiebreaker after total points.

Tiebreakers

In order: (1) total points for the competition period, (2) most zeroes — exact position matches, (3) fewest swaps used for the same score, (4) best single gameweek score, and (5) an internal identifier in the rare case everything above is tied. In practice, points and zeroes decide almost everything — the rest are footnotes.

Only as a tiebreaker, and only if you're tied with someone on points and zeroes. Swapping more gives you more chances to improve your table, but if two players end up dead even, the one who used fewer swaps to get there wins the tie — using swaps efficiently is its own small edge.
