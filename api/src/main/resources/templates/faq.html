Frequently Asked

Everything about swaps, entering the competition, and how scoring works.

Getting started

Everyone starts by predicting the order the Premier League table will be in after the current gameweek. Here’s how it works:

  • You don’t start from a blank sheet — you inherit a table and nudge it, tapping two clubs to swap them and then submitting when you’re happy.
  • Setting up, you get 5 swaps. Once you’re in, one every 24 hours.
  • This game is about damage limitation. You’re scored on how far each club sits from where you placed it — not on calling the table perfectly.
  • Swap scarcity is the mechanic that makes it bite. You can’t rebuild the whole table each week, so every swap has to be worth spending.

You can compete on the main leaderboard or in private leagues with friends, family and colleagues.

Aim for as few hits as you can — a hit being one point lost for each place a club is out by. A perfect table is very unlikely, and you don’t need one.

The damage comes from one club sitting a long way off, not from being slightly out. Have Liverpool 1st while they sit 14th and that single row costs you a 13-hit swing.

Play defensively: find the clubs sitting furthest from where you put them and spend your swaps closing those gaps, rather than chasing exact positions.

Look at the coming fixtures, not just the table as it stands — a club you have low, with a kind run ahead, may climb up, while one you have placed high can slide down.

You don’t have to guess which gap to close: the What-If page lets you test a swap before you spend it. See Planning with What-If below.

Swaps during the season

One swap every 24 hours. The clock is rolling, not fixed to a time of day — it starts counting down the moment you use a swap, and resets once that 24 hours is up.

Each time a new gameweek opens, you get an additional swap on top of your regular allowance — useful if you need to reposition more of your table than a single swap allows.

Nope! If you don't touch your table during a gameweek, it just carries forward unchanged into the next round — no update needed. You're more than welcome to submit your initial prediction and ride it out for the whole season if you like living dangerously... not recommended, but a perfectly valid way to play.

Two things stop you. The first is the cooldown: after you use a swap you wait 24 hours for the next one, so if that clock’s still running you can’t swap again yet — even with the round wide open.

The second is kick-off. Once a single gameweek’s match kicks off, swapping locks completely for that round — regardless of how many swaps you had left. It reopens when the next gameweek opens.

Scoring & grades

You start each gameweek on 200. Every club then takes a hit for each position it sits away from where you placed it — one position out, one hit:

Prediction Actual Points Hit
Arsenal in 1st Arsenal in 3rd -2
Everton in 20th Everton in 15th -5
Chelsea in 10th Chelsea in 10th -0

Your score is 200 minus your total hits across the twenty clubs. When the next gameweek opens, that score freezes and you begin again on a fresh 200. Your cumulative score across every gameweek is what the leaderboard ranks.

Every gameweek score earns a grade — chase it, even on a tough week.

A zero is a perfect prediction — a club you placed in its exact final position, 0 positions off, 0 hits. Zeroes matter beyond the points they save you: they’re also the first tiebreaker after total points.

Tiebreakers

1

Total Points

Your cumulative score for the competition period — sprint, quarter, or full season. Highest wins.

2

Zeroes — worth their weight in gold

Count of perfect predictions — position matched exactly (0 positions off). The more the better.

3

Fewest Swaps Used

Total swaps used during the phase. Fewer swaps for the same score signals higher efficiency and wins the tiebreaker.

4

Best Single Gameweek Score

Your highest scoring gameweek within the phase. Peak performance counts.

5

Internal Tiebreak

In the extremely rare case of a perfect tie across all criteria, an internal identifier is used. Focus on points and zeroes long before worrying about this.

The goal is simple: more points and more zeroes. Everything else is a footnote.

Only as a tiebreaker, and only if you're tied with someone on points and zeroes. Swapping more gives you more chances to improve your table, but if two players end up dead even, the one who used fewer swaps to get there wins the tie — using swaps efficiently is its own small edge.
